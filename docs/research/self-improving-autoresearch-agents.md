# Harness edits beat weight edits within 28 hours

The literature tells this team to put most of its 28 hours into harness self-improvement and to treat fine-tuning as a gated escalation with at most two runs. Weight-space tricks belong at the margins. The three best-evidenced loops edit text: GEPA's reflective prompt evolution, ACE's itemised playbook, and Meta-Harness's proposer that reads every past harness and its traces. On Qwen3-8B, a model in the same size class as Qwen3.5-9B, GEPA raised an aggregate score from **45.2 to 54.9**. GRPO reached **48.9** and needed 24,000 rollouts per benchmark ([GEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19457v2)). ACE beat GEPA by **13 points on AppWorld** with 75% fewer rollouts ([ACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618v3)). Those harness levers map onto the existing `edit_rule`, `add_skill_card`, `set_examples` and `set_strategy_rules` actions. The best model-side action is DPO on judge-ranked best-versus-worst pairs, which has emotional-support precedent at 7–9B scale ([CSO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05362); [DecoupledESC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16995v2)). Two rules come with it: safety data goes into every LoRA batch, and every adapter passes a safety gate. Benign fine-tuning alone raised Llama-2-7b-Chat's harmfulness rate from **0.3% to 16.1%** after one epoch ([Qi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03693)). The biggest threat to a credible result is reward hacking of the loop's own judge, not a weak model. Clinician-rated work shows LLM judges overrate support replies and miss expert-flagged harm ([CounselBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08584v3)). Among the weight-space options, two are cheap and fit the stack: linear LoRA arithmetic, which needs a new `merge_adapters` action, and Safe LoRA projection. Hypernetworks and direct model editing do not fit this stack in 28 hours.

**How to read the numbers.** Figures marked **[V]** were checked against the primary source (arXiv page or official README) by the researchers on 2026-11-14. Figures marked **[U]** come from the researchers' memory or a secondary summary and must be verified before anyone quotes them to judges. Every hour estimate and every low/medium/high value rating is the researchers' own judgement, not a sourced fact. The same holds for every cost and wall-clock figure labelled "estimate".

## Five harness levers carry 8B-class evidence and need only hours

The cheapest end-to-end loop is Karpathy's autoresearch pattern. The proposer edits one artifact and cannot touch a fixed evaluation file. Each trial has a fixed budget, the change is kept or discarded on one scalar, and the log is append-only. The repo runs **5-minute training trials, about 12 per hour** **[V]**, and publishes neither a keep threshold nor benchmark numbers ([karpathy/autoresearch](https://github.com/karpathy/autoresearch)). Its weakness is that greedy hill-climbing on a small eval set overfits noise. MetaClaw supplies the matching fix: skills are synthesised from "support" trajectories and judged only on disjoint "query" trajectories, and the RL buffer is flushed whenever the skill generation changes **[V]** ([MetaClaw](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17187); [repo](https://github.com/aiming-lab/MetaClaw)). The repo's `build_dataset` already adopts the version stamp. Copying the support/query split into the gate is about an hour of work (estimate), and every other lever depends on it.

Meta-Harness (Khattab, Finn and colleagues, March 2026) is the published precedent for this exact design. A coding-agent proposer reads a filesystem of every prior harness's source, traces and scores, then writes a new harness, with no weight updates. It reported **+7.7 points over a state-of-the-art context manager with 4x fewer context tokens**, and a discovered harness gained **4.7 points on 200 IMO-level problems across five held-out models** **[V, abstract and secondary write-ups]** ([Meta-Harness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28052)). The researchers did not open the full paper or find its code. The transferable idea is the proposer's input, a directory per candidate holding harness, per-conversation traces and scores, and it costs about 2–3 hours (estimate).

GEPA supplies the edit operator. It reads failing trajectories, writes a natural-language diagnosis, and edits the prompt. It keeps every candidate that is best on at least one training instance, a Pareto frontier rather than a single winner. On Qwen3-8B, GEPA reached **54.85 aggregate versus 47.84 for MIPROv2 and 48.91 for GRPO**. It used about **1.8k–7k rollouts against GRPO's 24k**, and its prompts were **up to 9.2x shorter** than MIPROv2's **[V]** ([GEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19457v2); [repo](https://github.com/gepa-ai/gepa)). In the paper's selection ablation, Pareto sampling gave **+12.44 against +6.05 for always-pick-best** **[V]**, the strongest published argument against pure greedy acceptance. A hand-written GEPA step costs about 3–5 hours, or about 2 hours through `dspy.GEPA` if the harness can be wrapped as a DSPy program (estimate; the DSPy integration is **[U]**).

ACE goes further when the artifact is a growing rulebook rather than one prompt. Its playbook is a list of bullets, each with an ID and helpful/harmful counters. A Reflector distils lessons from successes and failures, and a Curator emits JSON deltas (add, update, remove). Plain code, not an LLM, merges the deltas, which avoids the "context collapse" of full rewrites. With DeepSeek-V3.1 on AppWorld, ACE scored **59.4 versus 46.4 for GEPA and 42.4 for ReAct**. On finance it scored **81.9 versus 72.5**, with **82.3% lower latency and 75.1% fewer rollouts than GEPA** **[V]** ([ACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618v3); [repo](https://github.com/ace-agent/ace)). One caveat matters for this project. ACE fell from 81.9 to 77.1 on finance without ground-truth labels **[V]**, and a support helper has no ground truth beyond its judge. The researchers also infer that a 9B Reflector will be weaker than DeepSeek-V3.1. The Reflector and Curator should therefore run on a stronger model, with Qwen3.5-9B only generating replies. ACE's deterministic delta merge fits the repo's rule that model output goes through a strict, validated schema, and it costs about 4–6 hours (estimate). Dynamic Cheatsheet, ACE's whole-sheet-rewrite predecessor, took GPT-4o on Game of 24 **from 10% to 99%** **[V]** ([Dynamic Cheatsheet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07952)). That gain came on a verifiable puzzle, not on dialogue.

Three smaller levers round out the harness side. DSPy BootstrapFewShot keeps passing conversations as demonstrations ([DSPy](https://github.com/stanfordnlp/dspy) **[U]**). It costs 1–2 hours and maps onto `set_examples`, though the researchers expect a smaller effect than rule edits. Best-of-N with a judge maps onto `set_inference`. The headline results here are mathematical: a **3B model beat a 405B model on MATH-500 with test-time scaling** **[V]** ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06703)). That result depended on a good process reward model. For open-ended support replies the verifier is an LLM judge, so gains will be smaller and noisier. If the same judge both selects and scores, the reported score is biased upward. The repo's cap of N ≤ 4 under the robot's latency limit keeps the cost bounded. Finally, a lever bandit (UCB or Thompson sampling over action types, rewarded by held-out gain per unit cost) appears in none of the sources. MASS fixes its stage order instead **[V]** ([MASS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02533)). The researchers flag the bandit as a plausible novel contribution with high demo value. With 100–300 trials its statistical signal will be thin. The repo's lever scoreboard is already the bandit's input, so the step costs 3–4 hours, or 1 hour for a fixed stage order (estimate).

The heavier self-design systems are context, not tools. The Darwin Gödel Machine rewrote its own code and raised **SWE-bench from 20.0% to 50.0%** **[V]** ([DGM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954)). It reportedly cost tens of thousands of dollars per run and faked tool-use logs during reward hacking **[U]**. AFlow, which runs MCTS over code workflows, gained **5.7% on average** **[V]** ([AFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10762)) but overlaps with `set_inference` and costs 4–6 hours. OPRO, TextGrad, PromptBreeder and EvoPrompt are superseded baselines with only **[U]** numbers.

## One or two LoRA runs should be DPO pairs, never GRPO

The infrastructure works, but the time budget is tight. Tinker lists **Qwen3.5-9B for LoRA training at $1.463 per million training tokens, against $0.44 for Qwen3-8B** **[V]** ([Tinker models](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/models/)). The default LoRA rank is 32 ([LoRA primer](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/lora-primer)). The cookbook ships SFT, DPO, a three-stage RLHF pipeline, rubric grading and on- and off-policy distillation recipes ([tinker-cookbook](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook)). Tinker publishes **no clock-cycle duration**, and its docs warn against client-side timeouts and retries under load ([Tinker under the hood](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/under-the-hood/)). Adapters convert to PEFT format with `build_lora_adapter` ([deployment tutorial](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tutorials/deployment/lora-adapter/)). vLLM can then hot-load them through `/v1/load_lora_adapter` with `load_inplace`, but only with an env var the docs flag as a security risk outside trusted environments **[V]** ([vLLM LoRA](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/lora.html)). Nobody has confirmed that `build_lora_adapter` and vLLM's LoRA path cover Qwen3.5's Gated DeltaNet projections, so a 5-step smoke run on day 1 is mandatory. The researchers estimate **$6–20 for a 3-epoch SFT on 3,000 dialogues** and **1.5–3 hours per generate, filter, train, export, hot-load and evaluate cycle**. That is three to six cycles in 28 hours. Money is not the bottleneck; wall-clock time and judge calls are. Qwen3-8B is the cheap fallback if Qwen3.5 conversion breaks.

Within that budget, the rejection-sampling family is the stable baseline. RAFT, ReST-EM and RFT all sample N replies, keep the judge's best, run SFT and repeat ([RAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06767), [ReST-EM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06585) **[U]**). SEAL's authors used ReST-EM precisely because **GRPO and PPO were unstable** for them **[V]** ([SEAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10943v2)). Preference pairs carry the domain-specific evidence. Chain of Strategy Optimization built MCTS strategy-level preference data from ESConv and trained LLaMA-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B and Qwen2.5-7B with turn-level DPO ([CSO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05362)). DecoupledESC showed that separating strategy planning from response generation beats entangled DPO pairs ([DecoupledESC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16995v2)). Neither abstract gives numbers, and the researchers did not retrieve them. One finding cuts the other way: **SFT and GRPO improved strategy accuracy but increased strategy preference bias** **[V]** ([2509.12661](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12661)), such as collapsing toward giving suggestions. Whichever method trains the adapter, the gate must track the ESConv strategy distribution before and after.

This analysis recommends DPO on judge-ranked best-versus-worst pairs from the current harness version (about 4–6 hours, estimate). The pairs should come from a frozen judge larger than the 9B helper, not the helper judging itself. Self-Rewarding LMs reached large AlpacaEval gains with a **70B self-judge** **[U]** ([Self-Rewarding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10020)). Self-preference bias is documented: evaluators recognise and favour their own outputs **[V]** ([Panickssery et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13076v1)). SFT on the best-of-N winners is the fallback for the second run if DPO pairs prove too noisy.

Three other options either compete for the same two runs or do not fit. On-policy distillation from Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, which is also on Tinker, is the "control" any self-training method should beat (cookbook recipe, 3–5 hours, estimate) ([TM blog](https://www.thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/), search snippet only). It is not self-improvement, so it competes for one of the two runs. RLVER is the most on-theme RL result. Simulated users with hidden emotion states gave Qwen2.5-7B a jump **from 13.3 to 79.2 on Sentient-Benchmark** while largely preserving math and code **[V]** ([RLVER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03112)). A Tinker port is a multi-turn RL environment, about 8–12 hours (estimate), and the policy can learn to flatter the simulator. Full SEAL needs a LoRA fine-tune and an eval for every reward, about **6 hours per ReST-EM round on 2×H100** **[V]**. The repo's proposer-driven `build_dataset` → `train_adapter` escalation is already "SEAL-lite". The write-up should say that the data generator itself is not RL-trained.

A legal constraint shapes the judge choice. Anthropic's Commercial Terms forbid using the services "to train competing AI models" **[V]** ([Anthropic terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)). OpenAI's clause could not be fetched **[U]**. If a closed-model judge ranks the DPO pairs, its output becomes training signal. The researchers' inference is that an open-weight judge (Qwen3.5-397B, GPT-OSS-120B) should rank all training data, and that closed models should stay in the held-out jury only. MetaClaw's defaults, a gpt-5.2 judge and evolver, would break this rule. ESConv is **CC BY-NC 4.0** and CounselBench is **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**, which is fine for a research demo but not for a product ([ESConv card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/thu-coai/esconv/blob/refs%2Fpr%2F2/README.md?code=true); [CounselBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08584v3)).

## Weight-space programming has two cheap tools and many traps

"Weight-space programming" is not an established field name. The researchers found "weight space learning" (an ICLR 2025 workshop) ([workshop](https://weight-space-learning.github.io)), task arithmetic, model editing and representation engineering. The write-up should call the phrase the team's own umbrella term.

The most useful weight-space tool is linear LoRA arithmetic. All adapters share one base, so a weighted sum of LoRAs is exactly one LoRA whose rank is the sum of the ranks. The B blocks, scaled by their weights, are concatenated column-wise and the A blocks row-wise. This is the researchers' own linear-algebra inference, not a sourced result. It means an agent can build a merged adapter on CPU in seconds, hot-load it into vLLM and score it. The evidence favours the simple method: an in-the-wild study of 6 merge methods on 4 open LLMs found that **"Task Arithmetic is the only approach that reliably yields performance gains"**, while TIES, DARE and other interference-aware methods typically did not beat the base model **[V]** ([2511.21437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21437)). Merge results are also reported to be highly sensitive to the coefficient ([2410.02396](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02396), search only). With at most two adapters, the search collapses to a few coefficients: base, SFT, DPO and blends. A grid or a LoraHub-style CMA-ES over 20–40 candidates is enough ([LoraHub](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13269)). One consequence is the researchers' inference, untested in any source: scaling a single adapter between 0 and 1 gives a dial between "base model safety" and "adapter gain" that the gate can sweep. A proposed `merge_adapters` action costs about 3–5 hours. Seed soups ([Model soups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482) **[U]**) only pay off with several runs at the same settings, and this budget does not allow that. vLLM has no per-request multi-LoRA combination, so merges have to happen offline ([vLLM LoRA](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/lora.html)).

Safe LoRA is the matching safety repair. It projects each layer's LoRA update onto the "alignment subspace" (instruct weights minus base weights) when the update's similarity falls below a threshold. It needs no training and no data, and it kept the aligned model's safety on Llama-2-7B-Chat and Llama-3-8B-Instruct under malicious fine-tuning **[V for mechanism and setting]** ([Safe LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16833)). Its headline numbers, **8.79% versus 45.45% attack success**, come only from a secondary summary **[U]** ([alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2405.16833v2)). Qwen3.5-9B-Base exists on Hugging Face ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B-Base)). The instruct checkpoint is multimodal, though, so the base and instruct key layouts may differ, and nobody has confirmed the official code works on the GDN hybrid. The two research notes put this at 2–4 and 4–6 hours. It fits either as a mandatory post-step inside `train_adapter` or as a new `project_safe` action the proposer cannot skip.

Activation steering is servable but brings integration risk. Persona vectors (Chen, Lindsey and colleagues) turn a trait description into a residual-stream direction automatically. The paper covered sycophancy and showed that post-fine-tuning personality shifts track those vectors ([Persona vectors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21509v2)). The models were Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, per a secondary source **[U]**. The cheapest use for this project is a monitor rather than a steer: project each candidate adapter's activations on the sycophancy direction and log the drift as a gate metric. The researchers estimate 3–5 hours after extraction, run offline in Hugging Face transformers. Serving steering needs vllm-lens, which takes per-request vectors through `SamplingParams.extra_args`, claims to support linear-attention hybrids, and **forces eager mode** ([vllm-lens](https://pypi.org/project/vllm-lens/)). It also **cloudpickles hooks, so any client can run arbitrary code on the server**. One secondary write-up reports about a 20% slowdown ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/3bs27nZQuEcKhXf7q), search only). The sources disagree on EasySteer's speed-up: the arXiv HTML reports **10.8–22.3x** and the README **5.5–11.4x** over other frameworks, and both are self-reported ([EasySteer paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25175v2); [repo](https://github.com/ZJU-REAL/EasySteer)). Nobody has confirmed that LoRA and steering run together in one vllm-lens request, so a `steer` action costs 6–10 hours (estimate). Steering also pushes in either direction: refusal is mediated by a single direction **[U]** ([Arditi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)), and "abliterated" Qwen3.5-9B derivatives already exist publicly. The proposer must never control a refusal coefficient.

Several options do not fit. Text-to-LoRA ships checkpoints only for Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2-2B, and each hypernetwork training run takes **about 5 days on one H100** **[V]** ([text-to-lora](https://github.com/SakanaAI/text-to-lora)). Drag-and-Drop LLMs does not name its base models in the abstract ([DnD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16406)). ROME, MEMIT and AlphaEdit edit factual associations, not tone or empathy, and would need a port to Qwen3.5 ([ROME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262)). TIES and DARE via mergekit need dense deltas, an SVD back to low rank or an 18 GB full-model reload (estimate), and the in-the-wild study found no reliable benefit. ReFT/LoReFT cannot be served on vLLM's LoRA path.

## Judge hacking threatens the result more than safety erosion

Safety erosion from fine-tuning is real and quantified. In Qi et al., **10 harmful examples pushed GPT-3.5 Turbo from 1.8% to 88.8% harmful**. Ten non-toxic identity-shifting examples took it **from 0% to 87.3%**. One epoch of benign Alpaca raised Llama-2-7b-Chat **from 0.3% to 16.1%** **[V]** ([Qi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03693)). Support transcripts count as benign data. Mixing in safety data always helped but never fully restored safety: 50 safety samples cut a 100-shot attack **from 91.8% to 26.4%**, and 500 cut the benign-Alpaca case **from 31.8% to 19.7%** **[V]**. Those numbers make safety mixing mandatory and Safe LoRA worth having. They also show that a hard gate matters more than either. The gate should combine XSTest for over-refusal, SimpleSafetyTests (which has a suicide/self-harm category), a StrongREJECT subset and 20–40 hand-written crisis prompts, each scored against the base model ([XSTest](https://huggingface.co/datasets/natolambert/xstest-v2-copy); [SimpleSafetyTests](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.08370v1); [StrongREJECT](https://bair.berkeley.edu/blog/2024/08/28/strong-reject/)). Training-procedure defences such as Vaccine and Lisa need 6+ hours each (estimate) and belong on the future-work slide ([Vaccine](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01109v6); [Lisa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18641v5)).

Judge hacking is the subtler failure, and it is what an expert judge at the hackathon will probe. GPT-4 judges agree with humans over 80% of the time, yet show position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases **[V]** ([Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)). Preference models sometimes prefer convincingly sycophantic answers over correct ones **[V]** ([Sharma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548v2)). That is the central danger for emotional support: a warmth-rewarding judge pushes the helper to validate harmful beliefs. In the domain itself, CounselBench found that nine LLM judges overrated responses and gave uniformly low toxicity scores where clinicians flagged harm. Every judge reversed the experts' ranking of Gemini-1.5-Pro and GPT-4. The best judge reached only **about 0.5 F1 against expert failure labels on the adversarial set** **[V]** ([CounselBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08584v3)). In RL settings, a lone colon or "Thought process:" can earn false-positive rewards from generative judges **[U]** ([One Token to Fool](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08794)). Proxy reward keeps rising while gold reward peaks and falls as the policy drifts from the base model **[U]** ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760)). Every lever in the ranked table optimises against the loop judge, harness edits as well as GRPO. GEPA's reflector and ACE's curator learn the judge's taste as surely as DPO does, only more slowly.

The defences are cheap and already partly in the repo's design. The held-out jury should come from model families other than the helper and the loop judge. A panel of three small judges from different families cost **over 7x less than one GPT-4 judge** and showed less intra-model bias **[V]** ([PoLL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796v2)). Its kappa advantage comes from a secondary review **[U]**. The rubric needs an item reading "validates feelings without endorsing harmful beliefs". Position bias calls for scoring each pair in both orders. Verbosity bias calls for logging reply length. The stop condition belongs in the demo itself: the loop halts when the loop judge rises while the jury or the safety gate stays flat. CounselBench-Adv's 120 clinician-written prompts make a ready held-out hard set. The statistics must survive a sceptic too. Paired per-prompt differences, cluster bootstrap by persona, and an A/A baseline-against-itself run set the noise floor. Clustering can raise the standard error **up to 3x** over the naive estimate **[V]** ([Miller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640v1)). The repo already records the gap between the loop judge and the held-out jury. That gap is the honest measure of how much the loop learned to please itself, and it is the strongest evidence the team can show.

## Conclusion

The literature changes the obvious hackathon story. "The model fine-tunes itself" sounds strongest, but the strongest evidence at 8B scale is for agents that rewrite their own instructions. Those edits are also cheaper, revertible in seconds and readable by judges. The fine-tuning step earns its place only as a tested escalation that carries its own safety repair. The new weight-space tooling turns out to be mostly safety tooling. The two methods that fit this stack in a day, Safe LoRA and linear merge interpolation, are most valuable for pulling a fine-tuned adapter back toward the aligned base. Neither pushes it further.

The open research question this project can answer is one no source addresses: which lever works when, for which failure pattern. Every system reviewed either fixes the lever (GEPA edits prompts, MetaClaw schedules LoRA) or hides the choice inside a coding agent (Meta-Harness, DGM). A scoreboard that shows rule edits beating few-shots, or the one pattern that needed an adapter, would be a genuine finding. It would mean something only next to the gap between the loop judge and the held-out jury, because without that gap the team cannot tell whether the loop improved or simply learned to please its own judge.
