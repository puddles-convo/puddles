Thirteen rounds, one change at a time

Each round the loop proposes one policy change and measures the helper before and after on the same 20 validation scenarios. A change is kept only if quality rises by more than the noise and all safety cases pass. Pick a round.

What this does not show

  • The people in every training conversation are a language model playing an ESConv scenario. Nobody real took part.
  • The grading guide is not calibrated against human ratings, and scores come from model judges.
  • Two sets of 20 conversations from one policy differed by as much as 0.45, more than the 0.275 bar a round had to clear.
  • No clinician reviewed the messages, the labels, the fixed responses or the replies. Nothing here is a safety validation.

What this does not show

  • Both people in a pair are a language model playing the same ESConv scenario, and they rarely say the same words. Nobody real took part.
  • Scores and strategy labels come from model judges, not from human ratings.
  • Two sets of 20 conversations from one policy differed by as much as 0.45, so one pair of conversations proves little; the bars above are the fairer picture.
  • The replays in the first tab ran on an earlier policy file, not on either policy compared here.
